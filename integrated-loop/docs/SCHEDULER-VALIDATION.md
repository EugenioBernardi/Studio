# The thalamic reticular nucleus as a consolidation scheduler — validation record

Stages 38–40. Model: `src/spindlegate.js` (210 → 260 lines, dynamics unchanged since stage 38;
stage 40 added measurement functions only). Tests: `npm run test:scheduler`, `test:scheduled`,
`test:topographic`.

**Nothing in `cortex.js`, `hippocampus.js`, `loop.js` or `consolidate.js` was altered across the
three stages.** The scheduler is a licence placed around the validated stage-4 consolidation loop.
That constraint is the point: a claim about the thalamus that required retuning the cortex or the
hippocampus to work would not be a claim about the thalamus.

---

## 1. The claim, and what it displaces

Systems-consolidation models are cortex plus hippocampus. The thalamus is almost always absent
from them, and yet:

- sleep spindles are thalamic, generated by the reticular nucleus (RTN) ⟷ thalamocortical loop;
- spindles and slow waves are frequently **local, not global** (Nir et al. 2011);
- RTN sectors are topographically organised and **mutually inhibitory** (Pinault 2004;
  Crabtree 2018);
- spindle measures are the strongest physiological predictors of overnight retention in humans
  (Gais 2002; Schabus 2004);
- thalamic lesions produce an amnesia that is **clinically different** from hippocampal amnesia.

The proposal: RTN is not a relay in memory but a **scheduler**. Because spindles are local and
sectors compete, only some cortical columns are plastic in any given slow-oscillation up-state, so
consolidation is **serial and capacity-limited**, and memories compete for spindle slots.

## 2. The mechanism, in full

One term. Cortical columns are partitioned into RTN sectors; each sector carries a spindle
envelope driven by the ongoing slow oscillation, sustained by its own recurrence, and **suppressed
by every other sector**:

```
drive_s = P.drive·so·(1+bias_s) + selfGain·env_s − lateral·Σ_{q≠s} env_q
          − refracDepth·refrac_s + noise
env_s ← env_s + (dt/tau)·(drive_s⁺ − env_s)
```

A memory may write a cortico-cortical transition **only while a sector holding any of its columns
is above the spindle threshold** (`licensed()`). That is the whole gate. Set `lateral = 0` and
spindling becomes global and simultaneous — the standard assumption the module exists to test
against.

Parameters (`sgdefaults()`): `nSector 8 · fSpindle 13 Hz · fSO 0.9 Hz · dt 2 ms · drive 1.0 ·
selfGain 1.35 · lateral 0.90 (0.5 in stages 39–40's nights) · tau 35 ms · thr 0.30 · noise 0.06 ·
refractory 0.45 s · refracDepth 0.55`.

## 3. What is established

### 3.1 Locality and capacity (stage 38, 9/10)

- Concurrent spindling falls **8.0/8 → 2.1/8** sectors as `lateral` rises from 0.
- Up-state-conditioned co-occupancy ratio **1.00 → 0.70** — sectors exclude one another.
- Licensed consolidation events show a **capacity limit**: adding memories does not add throughput.
- Focal sector lesions remove consolidation **for their own territory only**.
- Unexpected and registered as such: a **partial** lesion *raises* consolidation in the surviving
  sectors (24.3 → 44.0) by removing their competitors — disinhibition, not sparing.

**A measurement trap fixed here, worth not re-deriving.** All sectors share one slow-oscillation
drive, so co-occurrence is inflated above chance even when sectors compete hard — the first version
of `coOccupancy` reported 1.82 for a plainly-excluding configuration. **Condition on the up-state.**

### 3.2 The schedule drives real synaptic weight (stage 39, 4/5)

Each `KC.writeTransition` call inside the validated stage-4 loop is gated on `licensed()`.
"Consolidated" therefore means what it should — cortex can walk the sequence without the
hippocampus — rather than counting licensed events.

- **Capacity limit in synaptic terms.** Per-memory cortico-cortical weight **0.534 → 0.288 →
  0.118** for 2 / 3 / 6 competing memories, while **total** weight stays flat **0.534 → 0.577 →
  0.592**. The night has a fixed budget and more memories divide it.
- **The lesion dissociation.** Weight 0.118 → 0.000 under both hippocampal and reticular lesion;
  replay **81 → 0** (hippocampal) versus **81 → 81** (reticular). The diencephalic case *rehearses
  normally and learns nothing.*

**Two design errors fixed here.** (a) The first sweep varied gate `drive`, which changes competition
strength *and* spindle amount together — occupancy *fell* as drive rose, giving ρ = −0.70. (b) The
first version wrote *every* licensed transition on *every* replay event, so memories never competed
and the capacity limit could not appear however the scheduler behaved. **One memory per ripple.**

### 3.3 Dose, not mechanism (stage 40 §3, 3/3, held-out seeds 16–25 not needed — seeds 6–15)

Stage 39 *asserted* that its marginal behavioural dissociation (1.20 vs 1.00 steps) was a property
of the dose. Stage 40 tested it. At 360 s and 3 memories, over 10 held-out seeds:

| condition | replay events | weight | cortex-only recall (of 3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| intact | 324 | 1.200 | **3.00 ± 0.00** |
| hippocampal lesion | 0 | 0.000 | 1.00 ± 0.00 |
| reticular lesion | **324** | 0.000 | 1.00 ± 0.00 |

The assertion was right. This is the record's cleanest statement of the dissociation, and it is not
something a cortex-plus-hippocampus model can produce: replay is *perfectly intact* in the
condition that learns nothing.

## 4. What is refuted — and this is the substance of stage 40

### 4.1 An unbiased scheduler is capacity-limited but FAIR (P1a, P4 — both failed)

**Registered (P1):** hold rehearsal exactly equal across memories (round-robin offering) and each
should consolidate in proportion to what **its own** sectors won; ρ > 0.5, with the other-territory
control ρ < 0.25.

**Result:** ρ_own = **0.33** — FAIL. ρ_other = −0.13 — pass. So the effect is at least local rather
than global arousal, but it is far weaker than predicted.

**Registered explanation (P4), and it named a number that could kill it:** if the shortfall is
attenuation — each memory wins only 4–6 of ~16 offers, a heavily tied and binomially noisy outcome
— then giving each memory more offers must raise ρ toward its asymptote.

| night (s) | offers/memory | licensed frac | ρ(own, frac) | ρ(other, frac) | CV across territories |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 90 | 16 | 0.256 | 0.30 | −0.10 | 0.097 |
| 360 | 65 | 0.258 | 0.02 | 0.06 | 0.060 |
| 900 | 162 | 0.256 | 0.17 | −0.22 | 0.040 |
| 1800 | 324 | 0.259 | 0.25 | −0.30 | **0.026** |

**P4 REFUTED.** ρ does not climb. The table gives the real reason: as the night lengthens the
spread of spindle availability **across territories collapses** (CV 0.097 → 0.026) while the spread
of the outcome stays at 0.080 — three times larger, and binomial. Competition plus the refractory
term make the winner fluctuate fast, and integrated over a night **every territory receives the
same share.** A scheduler with no bias source has no reason to favour one memory, and it does not.

> Stage 40 §1's per-sector CV of 0.122 is that short-run fluctuation over 90 s, **not** a standing
> bias. Reading it as one is exactly the error P1a rests on, and the check's label says so.

### 4.2 The claim that survives is sharper than the one intended

**Serial capacity and topographic selectivity are separable properties of the same scheduler.**
Capacity is intrinsic to lateral inhibition and comes free. Selectivity requires a **bias source** —
a cue, a salience signal, prioritisation.

Confirmed on **seeds 16–25, never used anywhere in this project**, with thresholds fixed from the
exploratory pass first (§5 is labelled exploratory and unscored because its territory-alignment
confound was found by inspecting output, not predicted):

| condition | licensed fraction across 5 memories (bias 0 → 0.5) | ρ(bias, fraction) |
|---|---|---|
| no bias | 0.140 0.149 0.125 0.140 0.153 | **0.10** |
| drive cueing | 0.071 0.135 0.314 0.405 0.367 | **0.84** |
| competitive cueing | 0.072 0.163 0.220 0.278 0.330 | **0.90** |

**The prediction for human data:** topographic spindle–memory correlations (Nishida & Walker 2007;
Bergmann et al. 2012) should be **weak in undisturbed sleep and strong under cueing or
prioritisation.** The scheduler alone does not produce them.

### 4.2b The difference score, tried because the literature uses it, and it does not help

Nishida & Walker (2007) found that offline motor-memory improvement correlated with spindle
activity in the **learning minus non-learning hemisphere**, and *not* with either hemisphere alone.
That is structurally the same contrast as ρ_own versus ρ_other here, so their analysis was run on
this data — post hoc, and labelled as such:

| outcome | seeds | ρ(own) | ρ(other) | ρ(own − other) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| consolidated weight (in-test, §2) | 6–15 | 0.33 | −0.13 | **0.30** |
| licensed fraction (gate only) | 6–15 | 0.30 | −0.10 | **0.26** |
| licensed fraction (gate only) | 16–25 | 0.26 | −0.28 | **0.27** |

**The difference score does not improve the correlation** — on either outcome, on either seed set.
Reported because it is the first analysis a reviewer would ask for, and because it is consistent
with §4.1: there is very little standing territorial variation for any contrast to pick up.

### 4.3 Time, not count (stage 40 §2)

ρ(own-territory spindle **occupancy**, weight) = **0.33**; ρ(own-territory spindle **density**,
weight) = **−0.29**. The gate licenses writes for as long as the envelope is up and is indifferent
to how many onsets that time is divided into.

This **diagnoses stage 39's failed spindle-density prediction without rescuing it** — that
prediction (ρ > 0.8, achieved 0.70) stays failed, and its threshold was set without accounting for
the resolution of the measure. The replacement is a directly testable human claim: **over task
territory, total spindle time should out-predict spindle count.**

One correspondence the model gets for free and did not aim at. Cox, Hofman & Talamini (2012) found
the spindle-density/retention correlation is **specific to slow-wave sleep** and absent in light
sleep, concluding the potentiating effect of spindles is tied to their co-occurrence with slow
oscillations. In this model the gate is *driven by* the slow oscillation (`P.drive·so`), so a
spindle outside an up-state licenses nothing — the SWS-specificity is structural rather than fitted.

### 4.4 Stage 38's TMR fork is withdrawn as a qualitative claim

Stage 38 concluded that **drive**-mode cueing leaves uncued items unharmed while **competitive**
cueing costs them — a clean fork with opposite empirical predictions. On sector-aligned territories,
measured on the consolidation licence, held-out seeds:

| | cued | uncued | uncued vs baseline |
|---|---|---|---|
| no cue | 0.125 | 0.146 | — |
| drive | 0.388 | 0.096 | **−34%** |
| competitive | 0.546 | 0.068 | **−53%** |

**Both modes cost the uncued.** What survives is quantitative: competitive cueing costs them more.
Harder to test in humans than the original fork, and more likely to be true. The discriminating
measurement is the **size** of the cost to uncued items in a cued night, not whether there is one.

**Why stage 38 got it wrong**, and it is a harness defect rather than a mechanism change: memories
occupied 10 columns while sectors hold 8, so neighbouring memories **shared** a sector and a
per-memory bias never produced a per-memory gradient — the shared sector kept whichever bias was
written last. Territories are sector-aligned in stage 40 §5–6.

## 5. Ceiling effects, stated because they bound the claim

`Wcc` saturates at 40 writes (`wCCmax 1.2 / lrCC 0.03`), and mean writes per memory reach 84 by a
1800 s night. **Beyond a few hundred seconds every memory is pinned at the cap and the weights
cannot express any topographic difference at all.** Scheduling shapes *what is kept* only while the
dose is limited — one night, more memories than slots, which is the ordinary case.

---

## 6. Things not to re-derive

1. **Condition co-occupancy on the up-state.** Shared slow-oscillation drive inflates
   co-occurrence above chance even under hard competition (1.82 for an excluding configuration).
2. **One memory per ripple.** Writing every licensed transition per replay event means memories
   never compete and no capacity limit can appear.
3. **Never sweep gate `drive`** to vary spindle amount — it changes competition strength at the
   same time, and occupancy moves the wrong way.
4. **Sector-align territories** before applying per-memory bias, or the bias is silently
   overwritten in shared sectors.
5. **Spindle occupancy, not density**, is the quantity the gate tracks. Global "was any sector on"
   onset counting also saturates once sectors are numerous — stage 39's density moved only
   37.5 → 45.2/min across an entire sweep.
6. **§1's short-run per-sector CV is not a standing bias.** The gate is fair over a night.

---

## 7. Open

- The cue/bias source is currently exogenous. Where it comes from — cortical up-state amplitude,
  salience, a neuromodulator — is unmodelled, and it is now the load-bearing part of the account.
- `fSO` is fixed; the model has no NREM cycle structure and therefore nothing to say about spindle
  refractoriness across a night or about N2-vs-N3 differences.
- The scheduler is coupled to `spindlegate.js`, not to the 19-column thalamocortical model that
  generates real spindles. Reproducing the 12–16 Hz waxing-and-waning envelope from that model and
  gating on *it* is the physiological version of this claim.
